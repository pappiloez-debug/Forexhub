What Is a Prop Firm Maximum Loss?

A prop firm maximum loss is the maximum amount an account is permitted to lose under a proprietary trading firm's rules. If an account reaches the applicable loss threshold, the trader may breach the trading conditions of the account.

Maximum loss requirements are commonly discussed in relation to funded trading accounts, prop firm challenges, evaluation accounts and simulated trading programs. However, the exact calculation can differ substantially between providers.

Some firms calculate loss limits from the original account balance, while others may use equity, balance, high-water marks, trailing drawdown levels or other methodologies.

Important: The percentages in this calculator are user inputs. They are not intended to represent the rules of a particular prop firm. Always verify the current terms and drawdown methodology of your account provider.

What Is Maximum Daily Loss?

Maximum daily loss is a limit on how much an account can lose during a defined trading day.

For example, if a hypothetical $10,000 account uses a 5% daily loss limit, a simple fixed-percentage calculation would produce a $500 daily loss allowance.

Maximum Daily Loss = Starting Balance × Daily Loss % Example: $10,000 × 5% = $500

The calculation above is intentionally simplified. A real firm's daily-loss methodology may consider equity, floating P/L, commissions, swaps, the previous day's balance or a specific daily reset time.

What Is Maximum Overall Loss?

Maximum overall loss represents the maximum permitted decline in an account according to the applicable account rules.

In a simple fixed-drawdown example, a $10,000 account with a 10% maximum overall loss would have a $1,000 loss allowance.

Maximum Overall Loss = Starting Account Size × Overall Loss % Example: $10,000 × 10% = $1,000

The simplified account threshold would therefore be $9,000.

This does not mean every prop firm uses a fixed $9,000 threshold. Some firms use static drawdown while others use trailing or equity-based calculations.

Maximum Daily Loss vs Maximum Overall Loss

These two limits are related but they serve different purposes.

Maximum Daily Loss

The daily loss limit controls the amount an account can lose within the firm's defined trading day.

Maximum Overall Loss

The overall loss limit controls the maximum permitted account decline over the relevant account period.

A trader can therefore remain above the overall drawdown threshold but still breach a daily loss rule. Conversely, a trader can remain within a daily limit while gradually approaching an overall loss threshold.

How to Calculate Your Remaining Maximum Loss

If your account has a fixed overall loss threshold, the remaining loss allowance can be estimated by comparing your current balance with the calculated threshold.

Remaining Overall Loss = Current Balance − Overall Loss Threshold

For example, if the simplified overall loss threshold is $9,000 and your current balance is $9,400:

$9,400 − $9,000 = $400

Under this simplified model, the account would have approximately $400 of remaining distance before reaching the threshold.

Remember: A real prop firm may calculate the threshold using equity rather than balance, or may use a trailing drawdown. Floating losses, commissions and other account conditions can therefore change the actual available buffer.

Prop Firm Loss Limit and Account Equity

Account balance and account equity are not always identical.

Balance generally reflects realized transactions, while equity can include the effect of open positions and floating profit or loss.

This distinction can become important when a prop firm uses equity to determine whether a loss limit has been reached.

Traders should therefore understand whether their account provider measures its loss limits using balance, equity, a previous-day value, a high-water mark or another calculation.

Fixed Drawdown vs Trailing Drawdown

Fixed Drawdown

A fixed drawdown generally keeps the relevant loss threshold based on a defined starting reference point.

Trailing Drawdown

A trailing drawdown can move as the account reaches new highs, depending on the provider's methodology.

Because trailing drawdown calculations can be more complex, traders should not assume that a simple percentage calculation represents their actual account threshold.

Why Maximum Loss Matters for Funded Traders

Maximum loss is one of the most important risk-management concepts for traders using proprietary trading accounts.

A profitable strategy can still violate an account's rules if position size, stop-loss distance or simultaneous exposure causes the account to cross a loss threshold.

Monitoring your available loss buffer before entering a trade can help you understand how much account capacity remains under your selected assumptions.

Maximum Loss and Position Size

Maximum loss should be considered before calculating position size. Your permitted account loss is not automatically the amount that should be risked on a single trade.

For example, if a $10,000 account has a theoretical $500 daily loss allowance, risking the entire $500 on one trade would leave little room for other trades, spreads, commissions or market volatility.

Many traders instead establish a smaller predefined risk-per-trade amount.

Maximum Loss and Drawdown Management

Maximum loss and drawdown are closely related concepts, but the exact terminology used by prop firms varies.

Drawdown generally describes the decline from a defined reference point, while a maximum loss rule establishes a boundary that the account must not cross.

A trader should know:

  • Starting account balance
  • Maximum daily loss
  • Maximum overall loss
  • Whether limits are based on balance or equity
  • Whether drawdown is fixed or trailing
  • Daily reset time
  • Whether floating P/L counts
  • Whether commissions and swaps are included
  • Any minimum trading-day requirements

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the maximum loss in a prop firm account?

Maximum loss is the amount an account can lose before reaching the applicable loss or drawdown threshold. The exact amount depends on the firm's rules and account type.

What is a 5% maximum daily loss?

In a simple fixed-percentage example, 5% of a $10,000 account is $500. The actual daily-loss calculation used by a particular firm may differ.

What is a 10% maximum overall loss?

In a simple fixed-drawdown example, 10% of a $10,000 starting balance is $1,000. The corresponding threshold would be $9,000.

Does maximum daily loss reset every day?

Not necessarily in the same way for every provider. The reset time and calculation methodology are determined by the prop firm's rules.

Does floating loss count toward maximum loss?

It can, depending on the firm's methodology. Some rules are based on equity and can therefore include unrealized profit or loss.

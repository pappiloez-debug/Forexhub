What Is ETF Return?

ETF return measures how much an investment in an exchange-traded fund has increased or decreased over a particular period.

An investor's overall return can come from two main sources: changes in the ETF's market price and distributions such as dividends.

Investment costs can also reduce the final return.

ETF Total Return Formula

Capital Gain = Ending Value − Initial Investment

Dividend Income = Shares × Total Dividend Per Share

Total Return = Capital Gain + Dividend Income − Fees

ROI = (Total Return ÷ Initial Investment) × 100

What Is Annualized ETF Return?

Annualized return expresses the overall growth of an investment as an equivalent yearly compounded growth rate.

A common compound annual growth rate (CAGR) calculation is:

CAGR = (Ending Value ÷ Initial Value) 1 ÷ Years − 1

This calculator uses the estimated total return, including the entered dividends and fees, when calculating the annualized return.

ETF Return Calculator Example

Imagine an investor purchases 10 ETF shares at $100 each.

The initial investment would therefore be $1,000. If the ETF rises to $125 per share, the investment becomes worth $1,250 before considering dividends and fees.

Initial Investment = 10 × $100 = $1,000

Ending Value = 10 × $125 = $1,250

Capital Gain = $1,250 − $1,000 = $250

If the investor received $3 per share in dividends, the dividend income would be $30 before taxes and other applicable costs.

ETF Price Return vs. Total Return

Price return focuses on the change in the ETF's market price.

Total return can include both price appreciation and distributions such as dividends.

As a result, an ETF's total return can differ from its price change.

How ETF Dividends Affect Returns

Some ETFs distribute income generated by their underlying holdings.

Dividends can provide an additional component of investment return. Investors may receive the distributions as cash or, depending on their account and broker, reinvest them.

This calculator allows you to enter total dividends received per share so they can be included in the estimated total return.

How Fees Affect ETF Returns

Investment costs can reduce the amount an investor ultimately earns.

Depending on the investment and broker, costs can include commissions, trading costs, account fees and other expenses.

ETF expense ratios are generally reflected in the fund's performance rather than entered as a separate transaction fee. Therefore, this calculator provides a separate fee field for additional costs you want to account for.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is ETF total return calculated?

Total return is estimated by combining capital appreciation with dividend income and subtracting applicable fees.

What is ETF ROI?

ROI measures the estimated return relative to the original investment and is expressed as a percentage.

What is annualized ETF return?

Annualized return converts investment growth over a multi-year period into an equivalent yearly compounded rate.

Do dividends count toward ETF return?

Yes. Dividends can form part of total investment return when they are received during the investment period.

Does this calculator include taxes?

No. Tax treatment varies by investor, jurisdiction and account type.

Does this calculator use live ETF prices?

No. You enter the purchase and ending prices manually. The calculator does not require a live market-data API.

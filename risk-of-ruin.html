What Is Risk of Ruin in Trading?

Risk of ruin is a risk-management concept that estimates the probability that a trader will lose a significant portion of their trading capital after a series of trades.

A trading strategy can have a positive win rate and still experience severe drawdowns if the trader risks too much on individual positions. This is why position sizing, stop-loss placement and consistency are important parts of a trading plan.

The Risk of Ruin Calculator on EZTradingHub is designed to help traders understand the relationship between win rate, average win, average loss and position risk.

How Risk of Ruin Is Calculated

Risk of ruin calculations can vary depending on the model and assumptions being used. This calculator provides an educational estimate rather than a guarantee of future trading results.

The calculator considers your estimated probability of winning, probability of losing, average win, average loss, risk per trade and selected ruin threshold.

Expected Value = (Win Rate × Average Win) − (Loss Rate × Average Loss)

A positive expected value does not eliminate the possibility of losing money. A trader can experience a long sequence of losses even when the strategy has a positive historical expectancy.

Key Risk Management Factors

Win Rate

Win rate is the percentage of trades that close profitably. A high win rate alone does not guarantee profitability.

Risk Per Trade

Risking a smaller percentage of your account on each trade generally gives your account more room to withstand losing streaks.

Average Win & Loss

The relationship between average winners and average losers has a major effect on trading expectancy.

Example of Risk Management

Imagine a trader with a $10,000 account who risks 1% per trade. A 1% risk means approximately $100 is at risk on a trade before considering slippage, commissions or other trading costs.

If the same trader increases risk to 5% per trade, a sequence of losing trades can cause a much larger drawdown. Recovering from large drawdowns can also require disproportionately larger returns.

This illustrates why risk management should be considered alongside strategy performance.

Risk of Ruin and Drawdown

Drawdown measures how far an account falls from a previous equity peak. Risk of ruin is concerned with the probability of reaching a predefined loss threshold.

For example, if an account falls from $10,000 to $8,000, the drawdown is 20%. Recovering from a 20% drawdown requires a 25% gain on the remaining capital just to return to the original balance.

This is one reason professional traders often focus heavily on controlling downside risk.

Risk of Ruin Calculator FAQ

What does risk of ruin mean?

Risk of ruin refers to the probability of losing a predefined portion of your trading capital based on assumptions about your trading performance and risk.

Is a high win rate enough to avoid ruin?

No. Win rate is only one part of trading performance. Average win, average loss, position size, trading costs and losing streaks can all influence account survival.

What is a reasonable risk per trade?

There is no universal percentage that is appropriate for every trader. Many traders use relatively small fixed percentages because doing so can reduce the impact of losing streaks.

Can risk of ruin be zero?

In practical trading, it is generally better to think in terms of reducing risk rather than assuming that risk can be eliminated completely.

Does this calculator predict my actual trading results?

No. It is an educational risk-management tool. Actual results can differ because market conditions, execution, slippage, commissions, strategy changes and trader behaviour can affect performance.

What Is Margin in Forex Trading?

Margin is the amount of funds that a broker may require you to set aside to open and maintain a leveraged trading position. It is not the same thing as the total value of the position.

Leverage allows traders to control a position whose total notional value is larger than the amount of margin required to open it.

Margin requirements can vary according to the broker, instrument, account type, leverage level, market conditions and regulatory requirements.

What Is Leverage?

Leverage allows a trader to control a larger position with a smaller amount of capital. It is commonly expressed as a ratio, such as 10:1, 50:1, 100:1 or 500:1.

Higher leverage can reduce the margin required to open a particular position, but it does not make the underlying position less risky.

If the position size is too large relative to the trading account, losses can accumulate quickly even when the required margin is relatively small.

How Is Forex Margin Calculated?

A simplified margin calculation is:

Required Margin = Position Value ÷ Leverage

Position value is generally related to the position size, contract size and market price.

The actual calculation can be more complicated when the account currency differs from the instrument's quote or base currency. Broker-specific contract specifications and margin rules should always be checked.

Forex Margin Calculation Example

Suppose a trader controls a position with a notional value of $100,000 and uses 100:1 leverage.

  • Position value: $100,000
  • Leverage: 100:1
  • Estimated margin: $1,000

This simplified example demonstrates the relationship between position value and leverage. The actual margin requirement can differ according to the broker and instrument.

Used Margin vs Free Margin

Used margin refers to funds currently allocated toward maintaining open leveraged positions. Free margin represents funds available for opening additional positions or absorbing losses, subject to the broker's rules.

As unrealized losses increase, available free margin can decrease. If an account reaches the broker's margin-call or stop-out thresholds, positions may be restricted or closed.

Is Margin the Same as Trading Risk?

No. Margin and trading risk are different concepts.

Margin represents funds required to support a leveraged position, while trading risk refers to the potential amount that could be lost from the position.

A trader could have sufficient margin to open a position while still taking excessive risk relative to their account balance.

Margin for Gold and XAUUSD

Gold trading can have different contract specifications from traditional forex currency pairs. The margin required for an XAUUSD position depends on factors such as contract size, price, leverage and the broker's margin rules.

Before opening a gold position, check your broker's current XAUUSD contract specifications and margin requirements.

Important Margin Warning

Always verify the required margin, leverage limits, contract size, minimum position size and other trading specifications with your broker before placing a trade.

What Is Risk-to-Reward Ratio?

Risk-to-reward ratio compares the amount a trader could potentially lose on a trade with the amount they could potentially gain if the take-profit target is reached.

For example, a trade with a 1:2 risk-to-reward ratio means the potential reward is twice the amount being risked.

Risk-to-reward ratio is a mathematical measurement. A favorable ratio does not guarantee that a trade will be profitable.

How to Calculate Risk-to-Reward Ratio

The basic calculation compares the distance between the entry price and stop-loss with the distance between the entry price and take-profit.

Risk-to-Reward Ratio = Reward Distance ÷ Risk Distance

For example, suppose a trader enters a position at 100, places a stop-loss at 95 and a take-profit at 110.

Risk = 100 − 95 = 5

Reward = 110 − 100 = 10

R:R = 10 ÷ 5 = 2

Risk-to-Reward = 1 : 2

Risk-to-Reward and Break-Even Win Rate

The theoretical break-even win rate can be estimated from the risk-to-reward ratio, assuming simplified conditions and ignoring trading costs.

Break-Even Win Rate = Risk ÷ (Risk + Reward) × 100

For a 1:2 risk-to-reward ratio:

1 ÷ (1 + 2) × 100 = 33.33%

This means that, under the simplified mathematical assumptions, the theoretical break-even win rate is approximately 33.33%.

Actual trading results can differ because of spreads, commissions, slippage, swaps, execution conditions, changing market prices and other costs.

Is a Higher Risk-to-Reward Ratio Always Better?

Not necessarily. A higher potential reward relative to risk can also mean that the take-profit target is farther from the entry price and may be harder to reach.

Traders should consider their strategy, market conditions, stop-loss placement, probability of reaching the target and trading costs rather than selecting a ratio in isolation.

Risk-to-Reward in Forex, Gold, Crypto and Stocks

Risk-to-reward analysis can be applied to many markets, including Forex, gold, cryptocurrencies, stocks and other instruments.

However, the monetary value of a price movement depends on the instrument's contract specifications, position size, account currency and broker or exchange conditions.

This is particularly important when calculating monetary risk for instruments such as XAUUSD, cryptocurrencies, indices and other CFDs.

Risk-to-Reward Calculator FAQ

What is a good risk-to-reward ratio?
There is no single risk-to-reward ratio that is appropriate for every trading strategy. A ratio should be evaluated alongside the strategy's probability of success, market conditions, trading costs and risk management rules.
What does a 1:2 risk-to-reward ratio mean?
A 1:2 ratio means that the potential reward is twice the amount being risked. For example, risking $100 for a potential $200 reward represents a 1:2 ratio.
What is the break-even win rate for 1:2 R:R?
Under simplified assumptions and before trading costs, a 1:2 risk-to-reward ratio has a theoretical break-even win rate of approximately 33.33%.
Can a high risk-to-reward ratio guarantee profit?
No. A risk-to-reward ratio does not guarantee a profitable trade or trading strategy. The market may reach the stop-loss before reaching the take-profit target.
Does risk-to-reward work for gold and XAUUSD?
Yes. The price-distance concept can be applied to XAUUSD and other instruments. However, the monetary value of the movement depends on the broker's contract specifications, position size and account currency.
Does the calculator include spreads and commissions?
The basic calculation compares the entered entry, stop-loss and take-profit prices. Actual trading results can be affected by spreads, commissions, swaps, slippage and other transaction costs.

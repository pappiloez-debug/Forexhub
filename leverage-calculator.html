What Is Trading Leverage?

Trading leverage allows a trader to control a position with a market value that is larger than the amount of capital committed as margin.

Leverage is commonly expressed as a ratio such as 1:10, 1:50, 1:100 or 1:500. The ratio describes the relationship between the position's notional value and the capital required as margin under the simplified calculation used by this tool.

Leverage can increase the amount of market exposure available to a trader, but it does not eliminate the possibility of losses. Losses are determined by the position's exposure and price movement.

How to Calculate Leverage

A simplified effective leverage calculation can be expressed as:

Effective Leverage = Position Value ÷ Account Equity

For example, if a trader has $1,000 in account equity and controls a $100,000 position:

$100,000 ÷ $1,000 = 100

Effective Leverage = 1:100

How Leverage Affects Required Margin

Under a simplified margin model, required margin can be estimated by dividing position value by the leverage ratio.

Required Margin = Position Value ÷ Leverage

For example, a $100,000 position at 1:100 leverage would require approximately $1,000 of margin under this simplified calculation.

Actual broker margin requirements can differ depending on the instrument, contract size, current price, account currency, broker rules, leverage tiers and other conditions.

Leverage vs Trading Risk

Leverage and trading risk are related, but they are not the same thing.

Leverage determines how much market exposure can be controlled relative to the capital used as margin. Trading risk depends on factors such as position size, entry price, stop-loss distance, instrument volatility and the amount of capital exposed to a potential loss.

Increasing available leverage does not automatically mean a trader must use more leverage. Position sizing and risk management remain important when trading leveraged products.

Leverage for Forex, Gold and Crypto Trading

Leverage is commonly encountered in Forex and CFD trading and may also be available for certain cryptocurrency and other derivative products.

The exact leverage available can depend on the broker, instrument, jurisdiction, account type, regulatory requirements and other conditions.

Gold such as XAUUSD can have instrument-specific contract and margin requirements. Cryptocurrency derivatives can also have different margin rules, liquidation mechanisms and leverage limits.

Leverage Calculator FAQ

What is leverage in Forex trading?
Leverage allows a trader to control a position whose market value is larger than the capital committed as margin. It is commonly expressed as a ratio such as 1:30 or 1:100.
What does 1:100 leverage mean?
In a simplified model, 1:100 leverage means that $1 of margin can correspond to $100 of position value. Actual broker margin calculations can differ.
Does higher leverage mean higher profit?
No. Higher available leverage does not guarantee higher profits. Profit and loss depend on the size and direction of the position and the price movement of the instrument.
Does higher leverage increase trading risk?
Higher leverage can make it possible to take larger positions relative to account capital. If position size is increased, potential losses can also increase. Leverage itself does not determine the loss on a trade; position exposure and market movement are important.
How much margin is required with 1:100 leverage?
Under the simplified formula used on this page, 1:100 leverage corresponds to approximately 1% of the position value as margin. Actual broker requirements may differ.
Can I use this calculator for XAUUSD?
The leverage concept can be used when analyzing XAUUSD, but actual gold margin requirements depend on the broker, contract specifications, price, account currency and applicable leverage rules.
Is leverage the same as margin?
No. Leverage describes the relationship between position exposure and capital or margin, while margin is the amount of capital required or reserved to support a position.

What Is Trading Risk?

Trading risk is the amount of money you are willing to lose if a trade reaches its stop-loss level. Many traders use a percentage of their account balance to determine how much they are willing to risk on an individual trade.

For example, if a trading account contains $1,000 and a trader chooses to risk 1% per trade, the planned risk would be $10.

Risk percentage should be determined according to your trading strategy, financial circumstances and personal risk tolerance.

How to Calculate Trading Risk

The basic monetary risk calculation is:

Risk Amount = Account Balance × Risk Percentage ÷ 100

For example, with a $2,000 account and a 1% risk level:

$2,000 × 1% = $20

The planned risk would therefore be $20 before considering trading costs such as spreads, commissions or slippage.

Forex Risk Calculator Example

Suppose you have a $5,000 trading account and decide to risk 2% on a trade.

  • Account balance: $5,000
  • Risk percentage: 2%
  • Maximum planned risk: $100

If the trade reaches its stop-loss, the intended loss based on the selected risk amount would be approximately $100 before spreads, commissions and other trading costs.

How Stop Loss Affects Trading Risk

Your stop-loss distance is an important part of position sizing. A wider stop-loss generally requires a smaller position size if you want to keep the same monetary risk.

Conversely, a tighter stop-loss may allow a larger position size while maintaining the same planned monetary risk.

Traders should determine their acceptable risk first and then calculate an appropriate position size rather than increasing position size simply because a trade setup looks attractive.

Does Leverage Increase Trading Risk?

Leverage allows traders to control a larger position with a smaller amount of capital. However, leverage can also increase the speed at which losses occur when position sizes are too large.

Leverage itself does not determine your planned monetary risk. Position size, stop-loss distance and the value of the instrument are important factors in determining potential loss.

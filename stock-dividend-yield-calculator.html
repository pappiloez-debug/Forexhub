What Is Dividend Yield?

Dividend yield is a financial ratio that shows how much annual dividend income a stock pays relative to its current share price. It is commonly expressed as a percentage.

Investors can use dividend yield as one of several metrics when evaluating income-producing stocks. However, dividend yield should not be considered in isolation because a high yield does not necessarily mean a stock is a better investment.

Dividend Yield = (Annual Dividend Per Share ÷ Share Price) × 100

Dividend Yield Calculation Example

Suppose a company has a current share price of $100 and pays an annual dividend of $4 per share.

($4 ÷ $100) × 100 = 4% dividend yield

If an investor owns 100 shares, their estimated annual dividend income would be:

100 shares × $4 annual dividend = $400 per year

Dividend Yield vs. Dividend Income

Dividend yield and dividend income are related but they are not the same thing.

Dividend Yield

Dividend yield is a percentage that compares annual dividend payments with the stock's current price.

Dividend Income

Dividend income is the actual amount of dividend money an investor receives based on the number of shares owned.

Annual Dividend Income = Number of Shares × Annual Dividend Per Share

For example, two investors can own the same stock and receive the same dividend yield, but the investor with more shares will generally receive more dividend income.

Is a Higher Dividend Yield Better?

Not necessarily. A higher dividend yield can sometimes be attractive to income-focused investors, but it can also be a warning sign.

For example, if a stock price falls sharply while the dividend remains unchanged, the mathematical dividend yield increases. This does not necessarily mean the company's financial condition has improved.

Dividend Growth and Future Income

Some companies increase their dividends over time. Dividend growth can potentially increase future income even if the number of shares remains unchanged.

This calculator includes an optional annual dividend growth assumption to provide a simple projection of future annual income.

Important: Future dividend growth is an assumption, not a guarantee. Companies can maintain, reduce, suspend or eliminate dividends.

Important Dividend Investment Risks

Dividend investing still involves investment risk. A stock that pays dividends can lose value, and dividend payments can change.

  • Dividends are not guaranteed.
  • Companies can reduce or eliminate dividends.
  • Share prices can decline.
  • High dividend yields can sometimes indicate financial stress.
  • Taxes can reduce your actual income.
  • Brokerage and transaction costs can affect returns.
  • Currency movements can affect international investments.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is dividend yield?

Dividend yield is the annual dividend paid per share divided by the current share price, expressed as a percentage.

How is dividend yield calculated?

Divide the annual dividend per share by the current stock price per share and multiply the result by 100.

Is a higher dividend yield always better?

No. A high dividend yield may result from a falling share price or an unsustainably high dividend. Investors should examine the company's financial position and dividend sustainability.

Does dividend yield change when the stock price changes?

Yes. If the annual dividend remains unchanged, yield generally increases when the share price falls and decreases when the share price rises.

Does this calculator use live dividend data?

No. The calculator uses the dividend and stock price values you enter manually.

Can dividend yield be used to estimate dividend income?

Yes, dividend yield can help estimate potential income when combined with the amount invested or number of shares. However, actual dividend payments can change.

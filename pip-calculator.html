What Is a Pip in Forex Trading?

A pip is a standard unit used to measure price movement in the foreign exchange market. For most major currency pairs, one pip represents a movement of 0.0001. For many Japanese yen pairs, one pip is typically 0.01.

Knowing the value of a pip helps traders understand how much a price movement could affect the value of a position. Pip value depends on factors such as the currency pair, position size, exchange rate, and account currency.

How Is Pip Value Calculated?

Pip value depends on the pip size, trade size and the relevant exchange rate. A simplified calculation is:

Pip Value = Pip Size × Position Size × Exchange Rate

The exact calculation can vary depending on the currency pair and the currency in which your trading account is denominated. Broker contract specifications can also affect the final result.

Pip Value Examples

EUR/USD – 1.00 Lot

For a standard 1.00-lot EUR/USD position, one pip is commonly worth approximately $10 when the account is denominated in USD.

EUR/USD – 0.10 Lot

A 0.10-lot position is approximately one-tenth of a standard lot, so one pip is commonly worth around $1 when the account is in USD.

EUR/USD – 0.01 Lot

A 0.01-lot position is approximately one-hundredth of a standard lot, making one pip approximately $0.10 in a USD-denominated account.

Why Pip Value Matters for Risk Management

Pip value helps traders understand how much money a position could gain or lose when the market moves by a certain number of pips.

For example, if a trade has a 30-pip stop loss and each pip is worth $1, the approximate monetary exposure before trading costs would be $30.

Traders can combine pip value with their account balance and planned risk percentage when determining an appropriate position size.

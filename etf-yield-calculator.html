What Is ETF Yield?

ETF yield is a measure of the income an exchange-traded fund may generate relative to its price or investment amount. For income focused ETFs, yield is commonly associated with distributions paid to shareholders.

A simple distribution yield calculation compares annual distributions per share with the ETF's current price per share.

ETF Yield Formula

ETF Yield = (Annual Distribution Per Share ÷ ETF Price Per Share) × 100

For example, if an ETF trades at $100 and distributes $5 per share over a year, the simple distribution yield is:

($5 ÷ $100) × 100 = 5%

Estimating ETF Income

Annual ETF Income = Annual Distribution Per Share × Number of Shares

If an investor owns 100 shares and the ETF distributes $5 per share annually, the estimated annual distribution income before taxes, fees, and other adjustments is $500.

Monthly Income Equivalent

Dividing estimated annual income by 12 provides a monthly income equivalent. This does not necessarily mean the ETF actually pays distributions every month. The actual payment schedule depends on the ETF.

How to Interpret ETF Yield

Higher Yield

A higher distribution yield can indicate greater income relative to the ETF's current price, but yield alone does not describe the complete investment profile of an ETF.

Lower Yield

A lower yield does not necessarily mean an ETF is unsuitable for an investor. Some ETFs may emphasize growth, diversification, or other objectives rather than current income.

Yield Can Change

ETF yield can change when the ETF's market price changes or when distributions change. Therefore, a historical or trailing yield should not automatically be treated as a guaranteed future yield.

Distribution Yield vs Total Return

Distribution yield measures income distributions relative to price or investment value. Total return can include both distributions and changes in the value of the ETF itself.

ETF Yield Calculator Example

Assume an ETF has a market price of $100 per share and distributes $5 per share over a year. The simple distribution yield is 5%.

If an investor owns 200 shares, estimated annual distribution income would be $1,000 before taxes, fees, and any changes to future distributions.

If the ETF's market price changes while its distribution remains unchanged, the calculated yield based on the new market price will also change.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is ETF yield?

ETF yield is a measure of distributions generated by an ETF relative to its price or investment amount. A simple distribution yield divides annual distributions per share by the ETF's price per share.

How is ETF yield calculated?

A simple ETF distribution yield is calculated by dividing annual distributions per share by the ETF price per share and multiplying the result by 100.

Does a higher ETF yield always mean a better investment?

No. Yield is only one factor. Investors may also evaluate total return, volatility, expenses, diversification, underlying holdings, distribution consistency, taxes, and investment objectives.

Can ETF distributions change?

Yes. ETF distributions may change depending on the income generated by the underlying investments and the fund's distribution policy.

Does this calculator use live ETF data?

No. This calculator uses the values entered by the user. It does not automatically retrieve live ETF prices or distributions.

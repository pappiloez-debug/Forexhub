What Is Stock Investment Return?

Stock investment return measures how much an investment has gained or lost relative to the amount originally invested. A complete return calculation can consider both changes in the stock price and dividends received.

For example, if you purchase shares for $10,000 and their value later increases to $12,500, you have a $2,500 capital gain. If you also received $400 in dividends, your total return before applicable taxes and other adjustments would be $2,900.

Total Return = Capital Gain/Loss + Dividends − Applicable Costs

Stock Return Formulas

Initial Investment

Initial Investment = Number of Shares × Purchase Price

Capital Gain or Loss

Capital Gain/Loss = (Current Price − Purchase Price) × Shares

Dividend Income

Dividend Income = Dividend Per Share × Number of Shares

Total Return

Total Return = Capital Gain/Loss + Dividend Income − Fees

Return on Investment

ROI = (Total Return ÷ Initial Investment) × 100

These formulas provide a simplified estimate. Actual investment returns can be affected by taxes, transaction costs, currency conversion, dividend reinvestment and other factors.

Price Return vs. Total Return

Price Return

Price return measures the change in the stock's market price without considering dividends.

Price Return = (Current Price − Purchase Price) ÷ Purchase Price × 100

Total Return

Total return considers both the change in the stock price and income received from dividends, along with applicable costs.

For dividend-paying investments, total return can therefore provide a more complete picture of investment performance than price appreciation alone.

What Is Annualized Return?

Annualized return expresses the growth of an investment as an equivalent yearly rate. It is particularly useful when comparing investments held for different lengths of time.

Annualized Return = (Ending Value ÷ Initial Investment) 1 / Years − 1

The calculation assumes compounding and is a simplified estimate when dividends are treated as part of the ending value. It should not be confused with an actual historical performance calculation that accounts for the timing of cash flows.

Why Dividends Matter to Stock Returns

Dividends can represent an important part of the return generated by some stocks. Two investments with identical share-price performance can produce different total returns if their dividend payments differ.

However, dividends are not guaranteed. Companies can increase, reduce, suspend or eliminate dividend payments depending on their financial position and business conditions.

Important: A high dividend payment does not automatically mean an investment is attractive. Consider dividend sustainability, earnings, cash flow, valuation and the overall financial condition of the company.

How Fees and Taxes Affect Stock Returns

Brokerage commissions, exchange fees and other transaction costs can reduce the return you actually receive.

Taxes can also affect the final amount available to an investor. The tax treatment of dividends and capital gains varies by country, investor circumstances and account type.

This calculator allows you to enter total investment fees, but it does not calculate personal taxes.

Important Stock Investment Risks

  • Stock prices can rise or fall significantly.
  • Past performance does not guarantee future results.
  • Dividends may be reduced or eliminated.
  • Investment fees can reduce actual returns.
  • Taxes can affect net investment performance.
  • Currency movements can affect international investments.
  • Individual stocks can experience company-specific risks.
  • Market conditions can change rapidly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is stock investment return?

Stock investment return is the gain or loss generated by an investment relative to the amount invested. It can include changes in share price and dividends received.

How is stock return calculated?

A basic stock return can be calculated by adding capital gain and dividends, subtracting applicable costs, and comparing the result with the original investment.

Does stock return include dividends?

A total-return calculation can include dividends received in addition to changes in the stock price.

What is annualized return?

Annualized return converts investment growth over a holding period into an equivalent yearly growth rate, assuming compounding.

Does this calculator use live stock prices?

No. This calculator uses the prices, dividends, fees and holding period entered by the user.

Does stock return include taxes?

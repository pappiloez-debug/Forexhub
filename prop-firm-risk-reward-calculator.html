What Is a Prop Firm Risk Reward Calculator?

A prop firm risk reward calculator compares the amount a trader plans to risk on a position with the potential reward at the chosen take-profit level. It can help traders prepare a consistent trade plan for an evaluation challenge or funded account.

The risk-to-reward ratio is commonly expressed as 1:2, 1:3 or another relationship between potential loss and potential profit. A 1:2 risk-to-reward setup means the planned gross reward is twice the planned gross risk before trading costs.

The ratio alone does not establish whether a trade is profitable over time. Results also depend on the strategy's win rate, the size of actual wins and losses, fees, slippage, execution and changing market conditions.

How to Calculate Risk-to-Reward Ratio

Reward-to-risk formula:
Reward-to-risk ratio = Potential gross reward ÷ Planned gross risk

Suppose a trade has a planned loss of $50 and a potential gross profit of $100. The reward-to-risk ratio is 2.0, commonly described as a 1:2 risk-to-reward setup.

If the estimated total costs are $4, the potential net profit becomes $96 and the net loss, assuming the same costs on a losing trade, becomes $54. The ratio of net profit to net loss would therefore be approximately 1.78. The actual cost on a losing or winning trade may differ.

Risk-to-Reward and Prop Firm Drawdown Rules

Prop firms can impose separate daily and maximum overall loss limits. A planned trade may have a defined risk-to-reward ratio and still be unsuitable for the remaining loss allowance in an account.

For example, a trader with $150 of daily loss allowance remaining should not assume that three trades with $50 of planned risk are guaranteed to fit within that limit. Spreads, commissions, slippage, gaps and simultaneous open positions may cause actual losses to differ from planned amounts.

The daily allowance estimate on this page uses the account balance, daily limit percentage and daily loss already used that you enter. It is a simplified model and does not replicate any provider's official breach calculation.

Understanding the Break-Even Win Rate

The break-even win rate estimates the percentage of winning trades required to offset losing trades when the win and loss amounts are assumed to be consistent.

Basic formula:
Break-even win rate = Average loss ÷ (Average win + Average loss) × 100

For example, if the average winning trade earns $100 and the average losing trade loses $50, the break-even win rate before costs is 33.33%. Trading costs generally increase the break-even rate.

This estimate does not guarantee profitability. Real trades may have different outcomes, and the actual distribution of wins, losses and costs matters.

Forex, Gold and Funded Trading Risk Management

Traders in forex, gold (XAUUSD), indices and other markets should confirm how their broker calculates tick value, contract size and position exposure. The monetary effect of a price movement depends on the instrument, contract specification, trade volume and account currency.

  • Check stop-loss distance before opening a trade.
  • Confirm contract specifications and tick or point value.
  • Account for commissions, spreads, swaps and slippage.
  • Review daily and maximum overall loss limits.
  • Consider exposure across correlated positions.
  • Do not treat a target ratio as evidence of a trading edge.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is a risk-to-reward ratio?

It compares the planned loss on a trade with its potential reward. A trade risking $50 for a potential gross reward of $100 has a reward-to-risk ratio of 2.0, or a 1:2 risk-to-reward setup.

2. How do I calculate risk on a funded account?

Multiply the applicable account balance by your chosen risk percentage. For example, 0.5% of $10,000 is $50. This is a calculation example, not a recommended risk level.

3. Does a 1:3 ratio mean I will make three times my money?

No. It means the planned gross reward is three times the planned gross risk if the target is reached. It does not predict whether the target will be reached.

4. What is the break-even win rate for a 1:2 trade?

Before costs, the mathematical break-even win rate is approximately 33.33%, assuming consistent wins twice the size of losses. Costs and differences in actual outcomes can change the result.

5. Can I use this for FundedNext challenges?

You can enter your own trade assumptions and compare the resulting estimates with the applicable FundedNext rules. This independent calculator does not verify compliance with any particular FundedNext program and is not endorsed by or affiliated with FundedNext.

6. Does this tool calculate forex lots automatically?

No. The price-based method requires the monetary value of a 1.0 price movement for the chosen position size. Use a suitable lot-size calculator and verify your broker's contract specifications separately.

7. Are trading costs included?

The calculator subtracts your entered estimated total costs from potential gross reward and includes those costs in its simplified net break-even calculation. Actual costs may vary by side, instrument and execution.

8. Does this calculator guarantee a prop firm pass?

No. It provides arithmetic estimates only. It cannot guarantee profitability, challenge completion or compliance with changing provider rules.

9. Is the calculator free?

Yes. The calculator runs in your browser and does not require a paid subscription or account registration.

Key Trading Terms

Planned risk

The estimated loss if the stop loss is executed at the assumed price, before any costs not included in the estimate.

Potential reward

The estimated gross profit if the take-profit level is reached.

Reward-to-risk ratio

Potential gross reward divided by planned gross risk.

Break-even win rate

The estimated percentage of winning trades needed to offset losses under the calculator's simplified assumptions.

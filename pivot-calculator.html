Pivot Point Methods Explained

Pivot points are mathematical price levels calculated from previous market data. Traders commonly use them as reference points for potential support, resistance, breakouts and intraday market structure.

Classic Pivot Points

Classic pivots calculate the central Pivot from the previous High, Low and Close. R1–R3 provide resistance reference levels while S1–S3 provide support reference levels.

Fibonacci Pivot Points

Fibonacci pivots apply commonly used Fibonacci ratios to the previous period's trading range. This produces support and resistance levels around the central pivot.

Woodie Pivot Points

Woodie pivots give greater weight to the previous closing price in the central pivot calculation.

Camarilla Pivot Points

Camarilla calculations use multiple range-based levels. The method is commonly used for identifying intraday support, resistance and potential breakout areas.

DeMark Pivot Points

DeMark pivots use the relationship between the Open and Close to determine the calculation branch. The method produces a central reference value and primary support/resistance levels.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are pivot points?

Pivot points are calculated market reference levels based on previous-period price data. They are commonly used to study potential support and resistance.

Which pivot methods are included?

This calculator supports Classic, Fibonacci, Woodie, Camarilla and DeMark pivot calculations.

Does DeMark require the Open price?

Yes. The DeMark calculation uses the relationship between the previous Open and Close to determine which formula is applied.

Can I use this calculator for gold?

Yes. You can use it with XAUUSD or other markets as long as you enter the appropriate Open, High, Low and Close prices.

Are pivot levels guaranteed to work?

No. Pivot levels are mathematical reference points. Actual market behavior can differ because of volatility, liquidity, news and other market conditions.

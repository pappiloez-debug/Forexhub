What Is a Prop Firm Profit Target?

A prop firm profit target is a specified profit objective associated with a proprietary trading evaluation, challenge or other trading account structure.

A profit target is commonly expressed as a percentage of the initial account size. For example, a hypothetical 10% target on a $10,000 account would represent $1,000 in profit.

The actual rules surrounding profit targets vary between firms and account programs. Some programs may have multiple stages, while others may use different objectives or no conventional profit target.

Important: This calculator performs mathematical calculations based on the percentages you enter. It does not represent the current rules of any specific prop firm.

How to Calculate a Prop Firm Profit Target

The basic formula is straightforward:

Target Profit = Starting Account Size × Profit Target %

For example, suppose the starting account is $10,000 and the target is 10%.

$10,000 × 10% = $1,000

The calculated profit target is therefore $1,000.

How Much Profit Do You Have Left to Make?

If you have already generated part of the target, you can calculate your remaining target amount by subtracting current profit from the total required profit.

Remaining Profit = Target Profit − Current Profit

For example, if your target is $1,000 and you have already generated $400:

$1,000 − $400 = $600

The remaining profit target is therefore $600.

Profit Target Percentage Calculator

You can also calculate how much progress you have made toward a target.

Target Progress = Current Profit ÷ Target Profit × 100

If your target is $1,000 and your current profit is $400:

$400 ÷ $1,000 × 100 = 40%

Your calculated progress would therefore be 40%.

Required Return for a Prop Firm Profit Target

The required return from the starting balance is normally equal to the selected profit target percentage.

For example, if the starting account is $10,000 and the target profit is $1,000:

$1,000 ÷ $10,000 × 100 = 10%

This is a percentage calculation only. It does not indicate how long the target should take or how much risk should be taken to reach it.

Estimated Trading Days to Reach a Profit Target

The calculator also provides an approximate number of trading days based on your entered average daily profit.

Estimated Trading Days = Remaining Profit ÷ Average Daily Profit

For example, if $600 remains and your assumed average daily profit is $200:

$600 ÷ $200 = 3 trading days

This is only a mathematical estimate. Markets do not produce a fixed amount of profit each day, and attempting to force a daily profit target can increase trading risk.

Profit Target vs Maximum Loss

A prop firm profit target should not be considered independently from the account's loss limits.

For example, a trader might have a hypothetical $1,000 profit target while simultaneously having a much smaller maximum permitted loss.

The objective is therefore not simply to calculate how much profit is needed. Traders also need to understand the relationship between the target, daily loss, overall loss and their chosen risk per trade.

Use the Prop Firm Maximum Loss Calculator to estimate account-level loss thresholds.

Profit Target and Risk Management

Reaching a profit target should not require abandoning a predefined risk-management plan.

If a trader increases position size substantially simply because a target is close, the resulting risk can become inconsistent with the account's loss limits.

A more structured approach is to establish risk per trade, stop-loss distance, position size and maximum daily exposure before entering trades.

Profit Target and Reward-to-Risk Ratio

Reward-to-risk ratio describes the potential reward relative to the amount being risked on a trade.

For example, a theoretical 2:1 reward-to-risk ratio means a $100 planned risk corresponds to a $200 theoretical target on that trade.

Theoretical Profit Per Trade = Risk Per Trade × Reward-to-Risk Ratio

The calculator includes this optional calculation for educational planning. Actual trade outcomes can differ significantly from the theoretical target.

Prop Firm Profit Target and Consistency Rules

Some trading programs may have additional requirements relating to consistency, daily results or payout conditions.

This means that simply reaching a numerical profit target does not necessarily mean every account requirement has been satisfied.

You can use the Prop Firm Consistency Rule Calculator to study largest-winning-day calculations and hypothetical consistency percentages.

Prop Firm Challenge Profit Target

During a proprietary trading evaluation, the profit target can be one of several conditions that a trader needs to monitor.

Depending on the account program, other conditions can include maximum daily loss, maximum overall drawdown, minimum trading days, consistency requirements, prohibited trading practices or other operational rules.

Traders should therefore avoid treating a challenge as a single-number profit objective.

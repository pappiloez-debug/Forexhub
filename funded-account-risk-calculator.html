Understanding Funded Account Risk Management

Daily loss limit

A daily loss limit restricts losses over a specified trading day. Providers may use the balance or equity at the daily reset, and may count floating P/L, fees and commissions. Confirm the provider's exact definition and reset time.

Maximum drawdown

An overall drawdown threshold defines how far an account may fall before breaching a rule. A fixed threshold may use the starting balance, while a trailing threshold may change as the account balance or equity changes.

Risk per trade

Risk per trade is the planned amount that may be lost if the stop-loss is executed at the expected price. Actual losses can be larger because of gaps, slippage, spreads, commissions and execution conditions.

Loss-streak planning

Modelling consecutive losses can help you understand how a fixed cash risk could affect an account. The illustration assumes the same planned cash risk on each trade. Real trading outcomes and position sizes can differ.

Why leave a safety buffer?

A buffer leaves room for trading costs and unexpected execution differences. The calculator's buffer is a user-defined planning allowance, not an official prop firm rule or guarantee against a breach.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is a funded account risk calculator?

It helps traders estimate cash risk per trade, daily loss allowance, remaining drawdown and potential profit based on their account details and chosen trading assumptions.

2. How much should I risk per trade on a funded account?

There is no universally appropriate percentage. Consider your strategy, historical losses, trading costs, account restrictions and personal financial circumstances.

3. Does daily drawdown use balance or equity?

It depends on the provider. Some firms include floating losses and fees in their calculations. Read the official account rules rather than assuming every firm uses the same method.

4. Can I use this calculator for gold or forex?

Yes, for illustrative cash-risk estimates when the monetary value per movement unit is correct for the instrument, trade size and account currency. Contract specifications vary between brokers.

5. Does the calculator guarantee that I will keep my account?

No. It provides planning estimates and cannot guarantee profits, prevent all losses or establish compliance with every firm's trading rules.

6. What does remaining drawdown mean?

It is the difference between the entered overall loss allowance and the drawdown amount you enter as already used. Your firm's actual calculation may differ, especially if it uses a trailing or equity-based threshold.

7. Why is my broker's position size different?

Pip value, contract size, account currency, permitted lot increments, commissions and execution can affect the effective trade risk. Verify your broker's instrument specifications before trading.

8. Is this calculator free?

Yes. EZTradingHub provides this calculator free for educational planning and risk estimation.

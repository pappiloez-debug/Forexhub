What Is Stock Profit?

Stock profit is the amount an investor gains when the value received from selling shares is greater than the total amount spent acquiring those shares and the applicable costs.

A simple stock return calculation starts with the difference between the purchase price and selling price. However, real investment returns can also be affected by commissions, transaction costs, taxes and dividends.

EZTradingHub's Stock Profit Calculator allows you to include these additional amounts so you can create a more useful estimate of the overall investment result.

Stock Profit Formulas

Initial Investment

Initial Investment = Shares × Buy Price

Gross Sale Value

Gross Sale Value = Shares × Sell Price

Dividend Income

Dividend Income = Shares × Dividend Per Share

Total Profit

Total Profit = Net Sale Proceeds + Dividends − Initial Investment − Other Costs

ROI

ROI = (Total Profit ÷ Initial Investment) × 100

Break-Even Price

Break-Even Price = (Initial Investment + Total Costs − Dividends) ÷ Shares

How Dividends Affect Stock Returns

Dividends can contribute to an investor's overall return in addition to changes in the stock price.

For example, an investment may produce a smaller price gain but still generate meaningful income through dividends.

This calculator allows you to enter total dividend income per share for the period being analyzed. For a more detailed dividend-focused analysis, use the EZTradingHub Stock Dividend Calculator .

Stock ROI Explained

ROI stands for Return on Investment. It expresses your estimated gain or loss as a percentage of the original investment.

A 20% ROI means that the profit is equivalent to 20% of the original amount invested, based on the assumptions used in the calculation.

ROI should be interpreted alongside the investment period. A 20% return over several years is different from a 20% return achieved over a much shorter period.

Why Trading Fees Matter

Trading costs can reduce investment returns. Depending on the market and broker, investors may encounter commissions, exchange fees, regulatory charges, taxes or other transaction expenses.

Even when a broker advertises zero commissions, other costs may still apply. Always review the pricing and fee schedule of the broker or investment platform you use.

Entering realistic costs into the calculator can give you a more conservative estimate of your potential net return.

Important Stock Investment Risks

Market Risk

Stock prices can rise or fall because of company performance, economic conditions, interest rates, market sentiment and other factors.

Company Risk

Individual companies can experience declining revenue, competitive pressure, management problems or other challenges.

Liquidity Risk

Some securities may have lower trading volume, which can affect the ability to buy or sell at a desired price.

Currency Risk

International investments can be affected by changes in exchange rates between currencies.

Dividend Risk

Dividends are not guaranteed and companies can reduce, suspend or eliminate dividend payments.

Tax Risk

Investment income and capital gains may be subject to taxes depending on your jurisdiction and circumstances.

Stock Profit Calculator FAQ

What does a stock profit calculator calculate?

A stock profit calculator estimates the gain or loss from buying and selling shares using the number of shares, purchase price, selling price, fees and optional dividends entered by the user.

How is stock profit calculated?

Stock trading profit can be estimated by subtracting the original investment and applicable costs from the net sale proceeds, while adding eligible dividends when they are included in the calculation.

What is stock ROI?

Stock ROI, or return on investment, measures the estimated profit or loss as a percentage of the original investment.

Does this calculator include dividends?

Yes. Enter the total dividend per share received during the period being analyzed.

Does the calculator use live stock prices?

No. This calculator uses the prices entered by the user. It does not automatically connect to a stock exchange or brokerage account.

Can I use this calculator for a losing stock trade?

Yes. If the selling price is below your effective break-even level, the calculator will show an estimated loss.

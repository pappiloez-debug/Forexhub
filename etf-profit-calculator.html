What Is an ETF Profit Calculator?

An ETF Profit Calculator helps investors estimate the potential profit or loss from an exchange-traded fund investment.

It can take into account the number of shares, purchase price, current or selling price, dividends and investment fees.

The calculator can then estimate the capital gain, dividend income, total return and return on investment (ROI).

ETF Profit Formula

Initial Investment = Number of Shares × Purchase Price

Final ETF Value = Number of Shares × Current/Selling Price

Capital Gain = Final ETF Value − Initial Investment

Dividend Income = Number of Shares × Dividend Per Share

Total Return = Capital Gain + Dividend Income − Fees

ROI = (Total Return ÷ Initial Investment) × 100

ETF Profit Calculator Example

Suppose you purchase 10 ETF shares at $100 per share. Your initial investment is therefore $1,000.

If the ETF later rises to $120 per share, the investment is worth $1,200 before considering dividends and fees.

Initial Investment = 10 × $100 = $1,000

Final Value = 10 × $120 = $1,200

Capital Gain = $1,200 − $1,000 = $200

If the ETF also paid $2 per share in dividends, dividend income would be $20 before applicable taxes or other costs.

ETF Total Return vs. Price Return

ETF price return measures the change in the ETF's market price.

Total return can also include distributions such as dividends.

For this reason, an ETF's total return can be different from its simple price change.

Why Dividends Matter

Some ETFs distribute income generated by the securities they hold. Investors may receive these distributions during the investment period.

This calculator allows you to include dividend income when estimating total return.

How ETF Fees Can Affect Returns

Investment costs can reduce the amount an investor ultimately receives.

Depending on the investment and broker, costs may include transaction commissions, spreads, account fees or other expenses.

ETF expense ratios are generally reflected in the fund's performance rather than being separately paid by the investor as a transaction fee. This calculator therefore treats the fee field as an additional user-entered investment cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is ETF profit calculated?

ETF profit can be estimated by combining the change in investment value with dividends received and then subtracting applicable fees.

How is ETF ROI calculated?

ROI is calculated by dividing the estimated total return by the initial investment and multiplying the result by 100.

Do dividends count as ETF profit?

Dividends can form part of an investor's total return. Whether they should be considered realized profit depends on the investor's accounting approach and whether the dividends were received or reinvested.

Does this calculator account for taxes?

No. Tax treatment varies by investor, jurisdiction, account type and investment. Taxes should be considered separately.

Does this calculator use live ETF prices?

No. This version uses the prices entered by the user. It does not connect to a live market-data feed.

Can I use this calculator for ETFs in different currencies?

Yes. You can select USD, NGN, EUR, GBP, CAD or AUD for displaying the calculated values.

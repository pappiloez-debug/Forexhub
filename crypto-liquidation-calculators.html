What Is a Crypto Liquidation Calculator?

A crypto liquidation calculator is a tool that estimates the price at which a leveraged cryptocurrency position could be liquidated if its available margin becomes insufficient to meet the exchange's requirements.

Traders use liquidation calculators to explore how leverage, entry price, position size, collateral and maintenance margin affect a position. These calculations are particularly relevant to perpetual futures and other leveraged crypto derivatives.

The calculator on this page provides a simplified mathematical estimate. Different exchanges use different formulas and account rules, so its output may not match the liquidation price shown by a trading platform.

How Is Crypto Liquidation Price Calculated?

Liquidation calculations vary by exchange and contract. This page uses a simplified linear-position model that treats the entered collateral as the margin available to the position and assumes a constant maintenance-margin rate.

For this model, define:

  • E: entry price.
  • Q: position quantity in coins.
  • C: available position collateral.
  • m: maintenance margin rate as a decimal.
  • f: estimated closing/liquidation fee rate as a decimal.

Simplified Long Position Formula

For a linear long position, the model estimates liquidation when collateral plus unrealized profit or loss is approximately equal to maintenance margin and the modeled fee reserve.

Estimated long liquidation price = (E × Q − C) ÷ [Q × (1 − m − f)]

Simplified Short Position Formula

For a linear short position, the corresponding simplified estimate is:

Estimated short liquidation price = (E × Q + C) ÷ [Q × (1 + m + f)]

These equations are educational approximations. They are not universal exchange formulas and do not model every fee, funding payment, maintenance-margin tier, liquidation penalty, mark-price rule or cross-margin wallet adjustment.

Why Exchange Formulas Matter

Some platforms use tiered maintenance margin, position-specific deductions, bankruptcy prices, liquidation fees and other adjustments. In cross margin, available account equity and unrealized P/L across other positions may also affect liquidation. Consequently, an exchange's displayed liquidation price should take precedence over this simplified estimate.

Isolated Margin vs Cross Margin in Crypto Trading

Isolated Margin

With isolated margin, a defined amount of collateral is assigned to a particular position. Under many exchange designs, losses on that position are primarily limited to its isolated margin, subject to the platform's rules and fees.

Use the isolated option for an estimate based on the collateral assigned to the position. This page does not reproduce any particular exchange's isolated-margin formula.

Cross Margin

Cross margin can use eligible account equity to support open positions. This may affect the liquidation level as balances, other positions, fees and unrealized profit or loss change.

The cross-margin option on this page is only a simplified approximation using the collateral value you enter. It does not connect to your exchange or inspect your wallet balance.

How Leverage Affects Liquidation Risk

Leverage allows a trader to control a position whose notional value is larger than the initial margin deposited for it. For a given position value, higher leverage generally requires less initial margin and leaves less room for adverse price movement before margin becomes insufficient.

For example, a position with a notional value of $10,000 at 10× leverage has a simplified initial-margin requirement of $1,000 before considering fees and other requirements. At 20× leverage, the corresponding initial-margin estimate is $500.

These figures illustrate the relationship between notional value and initial margin. They are not liquidation prices or recommendations to use a particular leverage level.

Can Liquidation Happen Before the Stop Loss?

Yes. If a position's liquidation threshold is reached before its stop-loss order closes the trade, liquidation may occur first. Stop orders are not guaranteed to execute at their trigger price, especially during fast markets, gaps or periods of low liquidity.

A liquidation calculator can help explore price distances, but it cannot guarantee execution or protect an account from slippage, outages or exchange-specific events.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a crypto liquidation calculator?

It estimates a potential liquidation price for a leveraged crypto position using assumptions such as entry price, position quantity, collateral and maintenance margin.

How do I calculate Bitcoin liquidation price?

Enter your BTC entry price, position quantity, leverage, available collateral, maintenance-margin rate and fee assumption. The calculator applies a simplified linear position model to estimate a liquidation price.

Is a long liquidation price different from a short liquidation price?

Yes. A long position is generally exposed to liquidation as the price falls, while a short position is generally exposed as the price rises.

Does increasing leverage increase liquidation risk?

Generally, yes. Higher leverage usually means less initial margin relative to the position's notional value, leaving less room for adverse price movement. Exact results depend on the platform and position configuration.

What maintenance margin should I enter?

Use the maintenance-margin rate applicable to your exact exchange, contract and position tier. The example value supplied by this calculator is illustrative, not a universal rate.

Is the calculator connected to a live crypto exchange?

No. The calculator runs in your browser and uses the values you enter. It does not fetch live prices, access exchange accounts or place trades.

Can I rely on this result as my exact liquidation price?

No. Actual liquidation prices can differ because of maintenance-margin tiers, fees, funding, mark prices, cross-margin equity and exchange-specific calculations. Check the official platform display.

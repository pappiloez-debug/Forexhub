Understanding Bond Yield

Bond yield measures help investors compare the income or estimated return of different fixed-income securities.

Current Yield

Current yield compares the bond's annual coupon payment with its current market price. It does not account for the difference between the purchase price and the amount received at maturity.

Yield to Maturity

YTM incorporates coupon payments, the current bond price, the face value received at maturity and the remaining time until maturity.

Bond Price & Yield

For many conventional fixed-rate bonds, market price and yield generally move in opposite directions. When price falls, the current yield generally rises, all else equal.

Current Yield Formula:

Current Yield = (Annual Coupon Payment ÷ Current Market Price) × 100

Annual Coupon Payment:

Face Value × Coupon Rate

Current Yield vs. Yield to Maturity

These measures are related, but they are not interchangeable.

Current Yield

Focuses primarily on annual coupon income compared with the bond's current market price.

YTM

Considers coupon payments, purchase price, maturity value and time remaining until maturity.

Why They Differ

When a bond trades above or below face value, the eventual gain or loss toward face value can affect its YTM.

Bond Yield Example

See how market price can affect a bond's current yield.

Suppose a bond has a face value of 1,000 and an annual coupon rate of 8%. Its annual coupon payment would therefore be:

1,000 × 8% = 80

If the bond trades at 950, its approximate current yield would be:

(80 ÷ 950) × 100 = 8.42%

This example illustrates why a bond's current yield can differ from its coupon rate when the bond trades at a premium or discount to face value.

Factors That Can Affect Bond Yield

Interest Rates

Changes in market interest rates can affect the prices of existing fixed-rate bonds and therefore their market yields.

Credit Risk

Investors may demand different yields depending on the perceived creditworthiness and default risk of an issuer.

Maturity

The amount of time remaining before principal repayment can affect a bond's price and required return.

Bond Yield Calculator FAQ

What is bond yield?

Bond yield is a measure of the return associated with a bond. Different yield calculations use different inputs and answer different questions about income or expected return.

How is current bond yield calculated?

Current yield is generally calculated by dividing the annual coupon payment by the bond's current market price and multiplying by 100.

What is YTM?

Yield to maturity is an annualized estimate of the return from purchasing a bond at its current price and holding it until maturity, subject to the assumptions used in the calculation.

Why can current yield be higher than the coupon rate?

If a bond trades below its face value, its annual coupon payment represents a larger percentage of the current market price. This can make current yield higher than the stated coupon rate.

Why can bond yield change even when the coupon does not?

A fixed-rate bond's coupon payment may remain unchanged while its market price changes. Because current yield uses market price in the denominator, its yield can therefore change.

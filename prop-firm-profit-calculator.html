What Is a Prop Firm Profit Calculator?

A prop firm profit calculator helps traders estimate the monetary outcome of a hypothetical trade or trading scenario. It can also help a trader measure progress toward a specified evaluation profit target.

The calculation is based on your inputs, such as starting balance, entry and exit prices, position value, trading costs and challenge target. The results are estimates rather than forecasts of future performance.

A profit target is only one part of an evaluation. A provider may also apply daily loss limits, maximum overall drawdown, minimum trading days, consistency requirements or other rules. Reaching a target in this calculator does not establish that an account has passed an evaluation.

How to Calculate Trading Profit

For a buy position:
Gross profit or loss = (Exit price − Entry price) × Monetary value per price unit

For a sell position, the price difference is reversed: (Entry price − Exit price) multiplied by the monetary value per price unit.

Estimated net profit or loss is calculated by adding your optional adjustment and subtracting estimated costs from gross profit or loss.

Net result:
Net P/L = Gross P/L − Trading costs + Other adjustment

These formulas assume the entered monetary value accurately represents the entire position. Realised results can differ because of execution prices, contract specifications, commissions, swaps and slippage.

How to Calculate a Prop Firm Profit Target

A percentage-based target is calculated by multiplying the starting balance by the target percentage. The target balance is the starting balance plus the target profit.

Target profit formula:
Target profit = Starting balance × Target percentage ÷ 100

Target balance formula:
Target balance = Starting balance + Target profit

For a $25,000 account and an illustrative 8% target, the target profit is $2,000 and the target balance is $27,000, assuming the target is measured from the initial balance.

Providers may define targets and balances differently, including rules relating to equity, closed trades, fees or other account conditions. Check the rules for your actual evaluation before relying on this estimate.

Trading Profit, Costs and Account Growth

Gross profit does not necessarily equal the amount added to your account. Trading commissions, spreads, swaps and slippage can affect realised results. Account-level charges and profit-sharing arrangements may also affect the amount ultimately received by a funded trader.

This calculator subtracts the estimated costs you enter, but it does not automatically apply a prop firm's profit split, withdrawal conditions or other charges. Add only the costs appropriate to the scenario you are evaluating.

  • Check contract size and tick value for your instrument.
  • Use realistic execution assumptions.
  • Account for trading costs where possible.
  • Keep profit targets separate from loss limits.
  • Review actual provider rules before making decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is a prop firm profit calculator?

It estimates potential trading profit or loss and can calculate the amount needed to reach a specified challenge profit target.

2. How do I calculate an 8% profit target?

Multiply the starting balance by 0.08. For example, 8% of $10,000 is $800. If the target is measured from the initial balance, the corresponding target balance is $10,800 before other adjustments.

3. Can I use this calculator for FundedNext?

Yes, you can enter the assumptions and target percentage relevant to your FundedNext program. This independent calculator does not verify compliance, guarantee a pass or represent an official FundedNext tool.

4. Does this calculator calculate forex lot sizes?

No. The trade-price method requires the monetary value of a 1.0 price movement for the full position. Use a suitable lot-size or pip calculator to determine position exposure, and verify your broker's contract specifications.

5. Does the calculator include trading fees?

Yes, you can enter estimated total costs. These are subtracted from the gross result, while any other adjustment you enter is added to the calculation. Actual costs can differ from estimates.

6. Does reaching the target mean I have passed?

Not necessarily. An evaluation may include drawdown, minimum-day, consistency or other conditions. The calculator measures a numerical target and does not check all provider rules.

7. Can I calculate a losing trade?

Yes. In trade-price mode, choose the direction and enter the exit price that reflects the loss. In percentage mode, enter a negative percentage for a loss.

8. Is this calculator free?

Yes. The calculations run in your browser without a subscription or account registration.

9. Does the calculator guarantee trading profits?

No. It performs calculations using the values you provide. It does not predict markets or guarantee profitability, funded status or withdrawals.

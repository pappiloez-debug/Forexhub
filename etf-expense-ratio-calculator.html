What Is an ETF Expense Ratio?

An ETF expense ratio represents the annual operating expenses of a fund as a percentage of its assets.

These expenses can include costs associated with managing and operating the fund. The expense ratio is normally reflected in the fund's net assets and performance rather than appearing as a separate invoice to each investor.

A lower expense ratio means less of the fund's assets are allocated toward operating expenses, although fees should always be considered alongside the ETF's investment strategy and other characteristics.

ETF Expense Ratio Formula

A simple estimate of annual expense cost is:

Annual ETF Expense = Investment Amount × (Expense Ratio ÷ 100)

For example, if you invest $10,000 in an ETF with a 0.20% expense ratio:

$10,000 × (0.20 ÷ 100) = $20 estimated annual expense

This is a simplified estimate. Actual fund expenses can vary because fund assets and expenses change over time.

Why ETF Expense Ratios Matter

Even a small annual expense ratio can have a larger effect over a long investment period because the money used to cover expenses is no longer available to participate in future growth.

The impact can be particularly noticeable when comparing investments with similar strategies but different ongoing costs.

Example: Comparing ETF Expense Ratios

Imagine an investor has $50,000 invested and compares two ETFs:

ETF A: 0.10% expense ratio

ETF B: 0.50% expense ratio

The simplified annual expense estimates would be:

ETF A: $50,000 × 0.10% = $50

ETF B: $50,000 × 0.50% = $250

The difference is $200 per year based on the starting investment amount. Over longer periods, differences in expenses can also affect the amount available for compounding.

Expense Ratio vs Other ETF Costs

The expense ratio is only one component of the overall cost of owning an ETF.

  • Expense ratio: ongoing fund operating expenses.
  • Bid-ask spread: the difference between buying and selling prices.
  • Trading commissions: fees that may be charged by a broker.
  • Taxes: applicable taxes on dividends, capital gains or other transactions.
  • Tracking difference: the difference between an ETF's actual performance and its underlying benchmark.

How Expense Ratios Affect Compounding

The calculator provides a simplified long-term projection using the entered return assumption.

Return After Expenses ≈ Annual Return Before Expenses − Expense Ratio

For example, if a hypothetical investment earns 7% before expenses and the ETF expense ratio is 0.20%, a simplified estimate would use approximately 6.80% as the return after expenses.

Actual investment performance does not necessarily follow this simple relationship because fund returns, expenses and portfolio values change over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an ETF expense ratio?

It is the annual percentage of fund assets used for the operating expenses of an ETF.

How do I calculate ETF fees?

A simple estimate is the investment amount multiplied by the expense ratio expressed as a decimal.

Is a lower ETF expense ratio always better?

Expense ratio is an important cost consideration, but it should not be evaluated in isolation. Investors may also consider the ETF's strategy, holdings, tracking performance, liquidity, risk and other costs.

Are ETF expense ratios charged monthly?

The expense ratio is an annualized percentage. Fund operating expenses are generally reflected over time in the fund's assets and net asset value rather than being presented as a separate monthly charge.

Does this calculator include taxes?

No. Taxes, brokerage commissions, bid-ask spreads and other transaction costs are excluded.

Can ETF expense ratios change?

Yes. Fund expenses and expense ratios can change over time. Always check the ETF's current official fund documentation for the latest information.
